\section{Introduction}\label{sec1}

% - Klinický kontext: EVT pri veľkocievnom uzávere, eTICI škála ako outcome, prečo je predikcia úspešnosti reperfúzie klinicky užitočná (voľba stratégie/zariadenia, informovanie pacienta/tímu, procedurálne plánovanie)

Acute ischemic stroke (AIS) is caused by loss of blood circulation in a specific area of the brain due to an occlusion of an intracranial artery and is one of the leading causes of mortality in the world \cite{Phipps2020}. The prognosis of the patient depends on, amongst others, the findings of neuroimaging, which consists of non-contrast computed tomography (NCCT) and computed tomography angiography (CTA).

Nowadays, possible treatment options for AIS include endovascular treatment (EVT), indicated in patients with large vessel occlusion (LVO), and intravenous thrombolysis \cite{Powers2020}.\NOTECHECK[However, there is currently no protocol to determine which treatment will be most beneficial for the patient, and the final decision is dependent on radiologists' experience.]{protokol je, není nástorj na odhadnutí..to vyzníva blbě} Since each patient with suspected AIS undergoes CT imaging, recent studies have been exploring the potential of leveraging CT-derived imaging features to improve treatment prediction. For this, extraction of appropriate image features from CT scans may be crucial to reveal the connection between the image and clinical outcome. The information about the amount of the reperfusion is quantified by the Extended Thrombolysis In Cerebral Infarction (eTICI) score, it has therefore been suggested as a suitable parameter for differentiating between the successful reperfusion (SRP) and non-successful reperfusion (nSRP). An estimate of the likelihood of reperfusion based on pre-treatment data could aid procedural planning and help maximize the benefit of EVT for the patient.

% - State of the art zameraný na: (a) radiomika trombu vo všeobecnosti,

Radiomics \NOTECHECK[\cite{Qiu2018}]{Gillies2016 nebo Aerts2014?}, a method enabling the extraction of a vast amount of information from image data, has garnered attention. This approach involves the extraction of numerous image features, including first-order statistics, gray level run length matrices, neighboring gray tone difference matrices, and shape-based characteristics of the region of interest, which are imperceptible to the human eye.
Hofmeister et al. \cite{Hofmeister2020} demonstrated on 47 patients the efficacy of clot-based radiomic features in predicting the mechanical thrombectomy strategy for successful recanalization of patients with AIS.
Qiu et al. \cite{Qiu2018} established a relationship between radiomic-based features and the efficacy of the recanalization with intravenous alteplase using NCCT and CTA images, along with the thrombus mask. These findings underscore the potential of radiomics as a valuable tool for extracting meaningful information from medical images, contributing to improved decision-making in the treatment of stroke patients.

% (b) konkrétne van Voorst et al. 2023 — čo urobili, aké AUC dosiahli pre eTICI≥2B (~0.52–0.56, teda blízko náhode), aký feature-selection použili (ICC≥0.75 filter, RF, žiadny ďalší výber príznakov)

In this context, van Voorst et al. \cite{vanVoorst2022} identified the association of thrombus volume and texture with the number of retrieval attempts needed for successful reperfusion, selecting features with an intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC)$\geq$0.75 and using the random forest classifier (RF). However, they did not observe any additional value of thrombus radiomics for the prediction of the reperfusion (eTICI$\geq$2B, AUC 0.52) compared to clinical baseline variables (eTICI$\geq$2B AUC 0.56).

% - Gap/limitácia predchádzajúcej práce: jedna pevná kombinácia feature-selection + klasifikátor; žiadne porovnanie granularity príznakov (voxel/distribution/global, whole vs. skeleton); žiadny permutačný test proti empirickému chance-baseline; žiadne overenie, či iná metóda výberu príznakov (Lasso/RFI) alebo klasifikátor (SVC) zmenia záver

Even though there are many studies which focused on the prediction of the clinical outcome using extracted radiomic features, to the best of our knowledge, \NOTECHECK[none of them exploited the potential of information hidden in the available data and used only the mean values of the extracted features from the thrombus.]{nemyslím si že je pravda} Moreover, none of the studies used supervised feature selection algorithms and based the feature selection purely on the removal of features which include similar information based on strictly defined threshold without further investigation.

Our study focuses on the exploration of features at different spatial scales, from global thrombus-level descriptors to single voxels. Moreover, we investigate the influence of the segmentation size on the prediction success.

This section establishes how much the choice of selector, classifier, feature set and region of interest alone can move discriminative performance within a single development cohort, before any of these results is compared with a null model or with the reference pipeline. Fig.~\ref{fig.AUC_ACC_distributions_all} shows the resulting distributions and \NOTETODO[S\ref{tab:S1-auc-acc-distributions}]{skontrolovat tabulku se statistikama do suplementu} lists the per-combination values.

Median cross-validated AUC across the 24 combinations ranged from 0.367 to 0.673, the four whole-thrombus global combinations being the highest as a group (0.632--0.673) and no centerline combination of either patient-level set exceeding 0.559.

Accuracy has to be read against the majority-class accuracy of the development cohort ($82/141 = 0.582$) and not against 0.5: only 4 of the 24 combinations exceeded it by median accuracy, and because the proportion of successful reperfusion varied between 0.448 and 0.714 across the outer folds, accuracy is meaningful only fold-wise.

Restricting the region of interest to the dilated centerline removed the discrimination present on the whole thrombus in the global-based set, where all four combinations fell from 0.632--0.673 to below chance (0.400--0.497); in the distribution-based set the effect was not consistent, the two combinations that had discriminated on the whole thrombus losing that discrimination while the two that had not changed little.

These results confirm the expected findings, that the information provided by a single voxel is insufficient to establish the clinical outcome. Nevertheless, this dataset was crucial for the creation of the global and distribution based datasets.

In the case of the models using the distribution-based information, the median AUC of the LASSO-RF model achieved only~37\% and median ACC was~50\%. \section{Methods}\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Study cohorts and outcome definition}

% 2.1 Study cohorts and outcome definition
% - Popis kohort (interná CV kohorta „NOIV" vs. externá „REGISTRY"), veľkosti, in/exclusion
For the purpose of this study, multicenter data from the MR CLEAN NO-IV trial dataset~\cite{Jansen2018}, including 539 patients with both NCCT and CTA scans and large vessel occlusion (LVO), were used to train, validate and test the models. Moreover, to increase the reliability of the study, the MR CLEAN Registry database~\cite{IvoGHJansen20180309} was used for feature reproducibility and model robustness testing.

% - eTICI≥2B ako binárny outcome, kto/ako labeloval

The patient exclusion criteria for both datasets are described in \cite{Mojtahedi2024}. As reported in \cite{Nemcekova2025}, an additional exclusion step was required due to the feature extraction process, which reduced the NO-IV dataset to 296 patients and the Registry database from 2597 to 1906 patients (16 missing TICI value, 575 missing segmentation, 100 spacing above 1 mm). Since the NO-IV dataset was used for model training, it was manually verified, and cases with inappropriate segmentation locations or multiple separated thrombus parts were excluded to ensure values from the valid image parts were used, reducing the training dataset to 141 patients.

The ratio of patients with successful reperfusion (SRP) to those with non-successful reperfusion (nSRP) was not adjusted, the final training dataset therefore consists of 82 patients with SRP and 59 patients with nSRP, preserving the same SRP to nSRP ratio as in the full dataset.

\subsection{Data preparation ...}

Since the database includes images from different scanners and therefore various resolutions, resampling the data to the same isotropic voxel size of~0.5~mm was crucial to ensure that the same amount of information is provided by each voxel. To prevent the impact of the marginal effects of the mask features and thus extract relevant feature values for whole thrombus, dilated segmentation mask was used for the feature extraction. A total of 1204 voxel-based features were extracted from both NCCT and CTA images using the PyRadiomics toolbox~\cite{VanGriethuysen20171031} with custom parameter settings described in the supplementary file \NOTETODO[PyradiomicsConfig.yaml]{PyradiomicsConfig.yaml -- not yet created}. Features with empty values were excluded before feature selection. This occurred due to the small size of the thrombus, which made the use of large filter masks useless.

According to our preliminary study~\cite{Nemcekova2024}, determining the borders of the thrombus is challenging, and even experienced radiologists do not fully agree on the size of the segmented thrombi. Uncertain thrombus segmentation boundaries may introduce unwanted information from the clot neighborhood, mixing information from two different tissues and potentially introducing noise into extracted features. To investigate the influence of segmentation size, and specifically the impact of edge voxels on the prediction, only voxels located on the thrombus centerline, dilated by 3 voxels, were selected, defining the \textbf{core thrombus area}.

To explore the feature potential of information contribution, extraction of three feature sets at different levels of neighborhood influence was applied: voxel-based, distribution-based and global-based. \textbf{The voxel-based set} included 2066 feature values for each thrombus voxel. The \textbf{distribution-based set} was derived from the probability density across 10 bins of thrombus feature histogram, which resulted in 20660 extracted features per patient. The \textbf{global-based set}, was based on global statistics computed from the feature values of the entire thrombus, including the voxel count, mean, standard deviation, minimum, maximum and the 25\%, 50\% and 75\% percentiles. Therefore, global based dataset included 16528 feature values per patient. As a result, six datasets (three feature set types and two region sizes) were used for the analysis.

To ensure that the division of the dataset into training and test sets did not affect the reproducibility of the predictions, the NOIV dataset was split into five non-overlapping training and testing folds. The definition of the splits in the context of the whole analysis pipeline is shown in the supplementary file \NOTETODO[XX]{doc/Thrombi_paper_FS_RF_SRP2.png}.

Each feature set underwent preprocessing, including z-score normalization based on the training set. The whole preprocessing pipeline is shown in Fig.\ref{fig.Thrombi_paper_FS_RF_SRP1}.

To define radiomic features that introduce an additional information for the diagnostic establishment and thus for the clinicians, the classification of the SRP/nSRP using the extracted features was performed. Since radiomics offers a large number of extracted features some of which may be redundant and may lead to model overfitting, feature selection was performed using two different methods. Feature selection using the Least Absolute Shrinkage and Selection Operator (LASSO) removed features with the coefficient value equal to zero. Second type of feature selection kept only predefined number of features with the highest XGBoost Random Forest Classifier Importance (RFI).

To ensure the selected classification method does not influence the outcome accuracy, a Support Vector Classifier (SVC) and XGBoost Random Forest Classifier (RF) were used.  All possible combinations of the selectors and classifiers were trained, resulting in four prediction method types (LASSO-SVC, LASSO-RF, RFI-SVC, and RFI-RF). This ensures that the definition of the most appropriate combination of the selector and classifier for this problem is established.

Parameter tuning was performed using Bayesian optimization with 20 trials. Feature selection and classifier training were treated as a single pipeline. Therefore, the Bayesian optimization was applied separately for each split, resulting in 120 trained models (3 dataset types x 2 area sizes x 5 folds x 4 methods), each with its own optimized parameter set and selected feature subset. The regularization parameter range for LASSO was defined according to Bickel et al., 2009 \cite{Bickel200981}. For the RFI selector, the number of features N was optimized, so that the top N features with sorted RFI values were selected. \NOTETODO[Table A1]{Detailed information about parameter setting and optimization needs an actual appendix table -- not yet created}. The final model for each combination of feature set, area of interest and method used was trained using the hyperparameters and selected features of the trial with highest accuracy during the optimization process. The whole process of model optimization and final model training is shown in Fig.\ref{fig.Thrombi_paper_FS_RF_SRP2}.

To support the green learning, only models for distribution and global based datasets were used for further model training and analysis, since all of the models using voxel based dataset predicted the same label. Thanks to the availability of REGISTRY independent testing dataset, optimization and training of models which used the whole NOIV database (all-fold models) for training was performed. This resulted in the creation of additional 16 all-fold models (2 dataset types - distribution and global based x 2 area sizes x 1 fold x 4 methods). To verify the possible predictive performance increase caused by the model combination, all combinations of models were provided to the boosting algorithm. Additional 16 boosted models were trained followed by the performance analysis.

All trained models are freely available and the framework is available on GitHub %\NOTETODO{PN}.
